    How alike do building and bombing sound?

    Taste of Cement follows Syrian men building a high-rise in Beirut while their own homes in Syria are bombed. This map turns its soundtrack into a space you can explore.

    What this is

    A curfew keeps the workers in the building's foundations after dark. The programme notes say they were not allowed to talk about their conditions, and the film barely lets them speak. Its sound carries the story: the cement mixer, hammering, a tank, shelling, the sea, silence.

    Each of the film's 5,098 seconds is placed by how it sounds, not where it was filmed.

    Method

    The soundtrack was measured every 23 milliseconds and averaged per second. A Google sound-recognition model, YAMNet, trained on about 2 million clips, named each sound. Its labels are grouped into tools and metal, engines and machines, bombs, voice, music, water, silence and wind. The analysis scripts are in the project's analysis folder.

    Limits

    • The model hears types of sound, not places. "Bomb" means it sounds like a blast, not that it was filmed in Syria. Use Places on the Timeline to record what's on screen.
    • It has no category for a tank or a cement mixer; both count as engines.
    • The audio is a compressed web copy, so the highest frequencies are missing.

    Taste of Cement is directed by Ziad Kalthoum; all rights belong to its makers. This site holds no audio or images from the film, only numbers measured from it.
