    Impacts

    Every sudden low-frequency hit in the film with no speech over it (a hammer, a slab, a shell, a boom). Each one is placed by three measurements:

    • Decay: how long the sound takes to fade by 12 dB. A short dry knock means the source is near; a long tail means space, distance and echo.
    • Weight: the share of energy below 250 Hz, which you feel in your chest.
    • Brightness: the spectral centre of gravity.

    Colour shows what a machine listener (Google's YAMNet AudioSet classifier) thought the hit was.

    What this is

    Taste of Cement follows Syrian men building a high-rise in Beirut while their own houses in Syria are being bombed. Lebanese curfews confine them to the building's foundations after dark. They are forbidden to speak about their conditions, and the film barely lets them speak at all. The soundtrack does the talking: the grinding of the cement mixer, hammering, a tank's tracks, shelling, the sea, long silences.

    This map treats that soundtrack as a territory. Each of the film's 5,098 seconds is a point. Its position is set by measurements of how it sounds, not by where it was filmed. The question the map asks is the one the film asks without words: how far apart do building and destroying actually sound?

    How to read it

    Sound space puts every second in 3D. Choose any three measurements as axes, or start from a preset. Rhyme lines join a machine-dominated second to the war-dominated second it sounds most like, judged by the classifier's 1,024-dimensional "hearing" of each moment. Click a point to set A, shift-click to set B, and listen to them one after the other.

    Timeline lays the same data out in film order. Impacts isolates the single hits: hammer against shell.

    Method

    Audio: the film's soundtrack (AAC, 44.1 kHz). The source copy is effectively mono, so stereo position is not measured. Signal measurements come from librosa on 23 ms frames, averaged to one value per second. Sound-type probabilities come from YAMNet, a MobileNet classifier trained by Google on 2 million YouTube clips across 521 AudioSet classes. YAMNet's classes are grouped into families: war (Explosion, Boom, Gunshot, Artillery, Thunder…), machine (Hammer, Jackhammer, Drill, Engine, Vehicle, Rumble…), voice, music, water, silence, air. All scripts are in /analysis.

    Limits, honestly

    • The classifier hears sound types, not places. A "war" label means sounds like a detonation, not filmed in Syria. Use the Places annotations to add where each shot actually is.
    • YAMNet has no "tank" or "cement mixer" class. Heavy machinery and armoured vehicles both land on Vehicle / Truck / Rumble. That blind spot is part of the finding.
    • The audio is a lossy web copy, so content above about 16 kHz is missing and is not used.
    • Thresholds (what counts as an impact or a cut) are choices. They are listed in the code.
